The lighthouse keeper had counted forty-seven storms that winter, and none of them frightened her anymore. She climbed the spiral stairs each evening with a thermos of black tea, checked the lamp, and wrote a single line in the logbook. "Wind rising," she would note, or simply, "Quiet." On the last night of February, a ship appeared where no ship should have been, drifting without lights toward the rocks. She reached for the radio, hesitated, and asked the empty room a question she had never needed to ask before. Who exactly was steering that thing, and why were they silent?
